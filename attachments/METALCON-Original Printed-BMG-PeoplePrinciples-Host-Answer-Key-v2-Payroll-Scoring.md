### BE-MK-01 — Principle 1: The Elongated Shadow

**Scenario:** You manage marketing for a basement waterproofing company. Lately the work coming out of your two coordinators is sloppy: typos on postcards, late social posts, missed deadlines. Truth is, you've been skipping your own proofing step and turning in your budget report late every month. The owner asks what's going on with your department.

**What do you do?**

A. Add a triple sign-off rule so nothing goes out without three approvals. \[$0\]   
B. Replace your weakest coordinator — somebody has to set the tone. \[$0\]   
C. Fix your own habits first. Proof your work, hit your deadlines, and let the team see it. \[+$500\]

**Correct:** C · **Why:** A department reflects its leader. The team is copying what you model, not what you say. New rules and new people won't fix a standard the leader isn't living.

**Principle Voice:** "Your business is your elongated shadow."

**Lesson Trace:** Editor-in-Chief; Pro-M 9; Visual Ambition

**Real-World Prep:** Before correcting your team's standards, audit your own.

### BE-MK-02 — Principle 2: You Deserve Your Results

**Scenario:** Spring leads came in 30% under goal. A Google algorithm update hurt rankings, and it rained out one home show. But you also mailed the postcards three weeks late and never tested the new landing page. Tomorrow you present results to the owner, and it would be easy to lead with the weather and Google.

**What do you do?**

A. Own it. List what you did and didn't do, then present your fix plan. \[+$500\]   
B. Report that the Google update caused the miss — it's technically true. \[$0\]   
C. Ask for a bigger budget to make up the gap. \[$0\]

**Correct:** A · **Why:** The marketplace gave you what you deserved. Blaming circumstances guarantees a repeat; owning the late mail and untested page is the only path to different results next spring.

**Principle Voice:** "The world and the marketplace is giving you exactly what you deserve right now."

**Lesson Trace:** Visual Ambition; Staying Above the Line

**Real-World Prep:** In every results review, list your controllable misses before any external factor.

### BE-MK-03 — Principle 3: Pain Is Fuel

**Scenario:** Your big home show booth flopped: 9 leads against a goal of 60\. You spent $8,000, staffed it all weekend, and watched competitors' booths stay busy. Monday morning you're embarrassed and the owner wants a recap. Part of you wants to declare home shows dead.

**What do you do?**

A. Recommend the company swear off home shows — the channel clearly doesn't work. \[$0\]   
B. Run a full debrief — booth location, signage, staffing, scripts — and rebuild the show system before the next one. \[+$500\]   
C. Tell the owner the promoter's foot traffic was weak this year. \[$0\]

**Correct:** B · **Why:** A setback is poison or fuel — you choose. Competitors' busy booths prove the channel works; your system failed. Turning the flop into a corrected system is how the pain pays you back.

**Principle Voice:** "The story you tell yourself about your past should be fuel, not poison."

**Lesson Trace:** Editor-in-Chief; Home Shows; Staying Above the Line

**Real-World Prep:** After any failed campaign, write a one-page debrief within 48 hours: what happened, why, and the three system changes you'll make before repeating it.
